On a Tuesday in June, a letter arrived at Softibank from Dakar. Amina recognised the envelope before she opened it. A revision to provisioning ratios this time, effective in sixty days, with the usual annexes.

She read it twice, out of habit. Then she wrote the proposal: a refinement of the rule, under compliance authority. She scheduled the review for Thursday and put the letter in the drawer.

That is the Teaching Edition's coda, kept in substance. The command she typed there, which is not built, and the cascade it returned, which is designed, are left out. What would happen on Thursday is what this book has run. The rule moves only when the code is under it, and the gate refuses the order that would break a declared rule. The pricing agent, an AI agent, may propose and may not move the regulation. The governance records each decision, and the file it is saved to can be read by an auditor eighteen months later. And the review on Thursday would read the cascade: today the points on each line, tomorrow everything the rule reaches.

It is not that the letters stopped mattering. It is that they stopped being emergencies. The distance between those two sentences is what this book was about.

The bank and its people are invented, and I said so in chapter 1. The discipline is not. Every refusal in these pages came from a library you can run, at the commit this book names, and every limit is written where it stops. What you build on it is yours. Stop trying to govern the intelligence, and govern the change.
