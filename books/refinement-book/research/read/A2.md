# Reading A2: Part I, read as the decision-maker

Reader: group A2, a bank's head of IT or of compliance; I read the prose and the printed answers, I do not run the code. Pages read whole, in order, twice. Nothing was run. No page in this group carries the six doors of the brief, so item 7 is answered as "no door on this page" each time, with what the decision-maker's entrance actually was.

## 01-the-letter.md

1. **What I remember.** A routine directive (cap from 18 to 16 per cent, ninety days) touches far more than one line, and the question is "what else moves". Limit: no saved history, and the cascade beyond a line is designed.
2. **Where I lost the thread.** Under **The slip**: "Suppose the proposal arrives with two digits swapped:" ends on a colon, and what follows is not the slip but a paragraph on the scene form, the classic form and the chain. The story stops for syntax at its tensest beat, and *Start here* already named the three forms. I would move that paragraph before the first block, or cut it to one clause after the block.
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - The `#-->` line "(structural + constraint + derivation + governance + trust passed)" next to "only the first two had anything to judge". The page is honest about it, but a compliance head reads a log line, not the paragraph under it: "governance passed" where no governance was declared is the sentence an auditor would ask about. The author may want one sentence saying what a reader of the log alone would conclude (chapter 5 may already do it).
   - "I put one sentence on the back cover of this book": the book is a web reader and a PDF; is there a back cover? A particular I cannot check.
   - "The city and the currency are real": the currency is never named on the page. Either name it or drop "and the currency".
   - Front matter `built` lists "the cascade of a point (its line) and of a class (the code graph)"; the page never runs a cascade, and **The limit** puts the cascade under "What is designed" while saying two cascades are built. The three statements agree on inspection, but a decision-maker reads the limit as "the cascade is designed".
4. **Moved.** "Nobody had to read a diff to see that sixty-one per cent is not a penalty cap a bank may charge." That is the page's argument in one sentence I could repeat to a board. And "The origin is recorded, not judged." Bored: **The court** (three guard names mean nothing to me; one sentence would do for my door).

6. **Errors.** "the trust floor" in **The court** against "floor of trust" twice above: one spelling. "the timeline with its checkpoints": checkpoints are not defined on this page.
7. **Door.** No door. My entrance was **The field** and **The principle**: "A diff tells you which lines changed. It does not tell you that the line was meant to change" names the four questions an auditor asks. Worth the walk.
8. **Verdict.** ready after the items above: move the syntax paragraph out of the slip, settle "back cover" and "currency".

## 02-what-broke.md

1. **What I remember.** Writing became cheap; the instruments of trust (diff, pull request, sign-off, version control) assume human speed, so approval drifts from understanding. Trust has to rest on the change: validated, scoped, attributed, reversible, explainable, none of which mentions the author. A four-point program shows each of the five answered by the library; the limit is that the reason is kept only at the moment of judgement and the history is not saved.
2. **Where I lost the thread.** In **The court**, "the answer this chapter reads as scope" took me two reads. And in **In your hands**, "`Why()` answers only for the last proposal": `Why()` has not appeared before; the blocks read `r[:why]`.
3. **Where I doubted.**
   - "Five properties, and none of them came from reading a diff." (under **The five, carried**). True, but read beside the list's own definitions it sounds larger than the run: *scoped* was defined as "what it reaches is computed and shown, not discovered in production", and the run shows the other point on the same line; *explainable* was defined as "can say, later, why", and the limit says the step does not keep the reason. The page states both stages honestly in **The limit**; I would say the stage in the paragraph itself ("scoped, on its line today; explainable, at the moment of judgement") so the claim and the proof meet in one place. Stage and direction, not a weakness.
   - Attribution. "the step keeps the origin the proposal declared" and, in the exercise, "an origin the library was never told about is ranked as trusted". For a head of compliance this is the first question: the origin is declared by the proposer, not established. The page says it plainly, which I respect; it needs a forward pointer to where origin is bound to who may declare it (chapter 8?), or the reader leaves Part I believing attribution is self-declared for good.
   - "In my view" three times in three paragraphs (**The afternoon**, then **What I refused**), plus "These are views drawn from the work I see, not measured results." One disclaimer would carry all of them.
4. **Moved.** "The name is still under the change. It certifies less." And the auditor's three questions: "who approved it, what was checked, and what it reached." That paragraph is where a bank's management stops skimming. Bored: nothing; this is the strongest page of the group for my door.
5. **The code.** Both blocks announced and read back line by line against the list; the read-back under **The five, carried** is the best use of `#-->` in the group.
6. **Errors.** Under **Where the field leads**: "The timeline you just read lives in the running object; the next paragraph says so." The next paragraph is "What is built"; the saved history comes two paragraphs later, under **The limit**. Say "the limit says so".
7. **Door.** No door. My entrance was **The principle**, bold. Worth the walk.
8. **Verdict.** ready after the items above: the stage stated inside "Five properties", the pointer for attribution, the "next paragraph".

## 03-the-unit-of-change.md

1. **What I remember.** The unit moved from the line to the diff to the refinement. A text patch sets the cap to `0.61` and nothing refuses it, no step is recorded: the diff's blind spot, run. Limit: other kinds pass unchecked, and the gate judges proposals, not the code as it stands.
2. **Where I lost the thread.** **The limit**, "What is absent": three gaps in one paragraph (other kinds unchecked; a text-edited value not flagged and becoming the value to return to; a non-number stopping the program), joined by "And" and "One more:". The second is the one a bank must know and it is buried in the middle. I would give it its own sentence at the head.
3. **Where I doubted.** Under **What I refused**: the discipline "leaves the other path visible as what it is". The first block shows the opposite inside the library: `NumberOfSteps()` is `0`, and the limit says the out-of-bounds value "is not flagged when the code is read again". Visible where, then? In version control, I assume. Say so, or the refusal reads as a claim the run contradicts. This is the item a compliance reader would underline.
4. **Moved.** "The two changes looked the same as text. As proposals, one is a refusal and one is a step." The first block's reveal, "nothing refused it!", is earned. Bored: **Where the field leads** repeats chapter 2's "the field leads in reach" almost word for word ("A diff can change anything").
5. **The code.** All three runs are announced and read. The `statements` library point is the one block a decision-maker grasps at once (swap the engine that prints customer statements); the court says it is not yet guarded, which is fair.
6. **Errors.** Under **The field**: "Chapter 2 named its limit. A diff records which characters moved. Those four were named in chapter 1, under **The field** ("A diff tells you which lines changed..."); chapter 2 named the instruments' assumptions. "Take the late-fee line of chapter 1": chapter 1's line is `penalty = ...`; `late_fee` is chapter 2's. "three times the ceiling": 0.61 is just over three times 0.20; acceptable.
7. **Door.** No door. My entrance was **The same change, typed** and the sentence after it. Worth the walk.
8. **Verdict.** ready after the items above: the chapter 1/2 reference, "visible" against "not flagged", the absent paragraph split.

## The group

**The thread.** Each bridge lands: chapter 1 closes on "why one change became hard", chapter 2 opens on the afternoon; chapter 2 closes on "why the replacement had to be typed", chapter 3 opens on the unit. Part I feels like a part: the letter, the cause, the unit, and *Start here*'s list of parts matches the pages. The bold definition of a refinement appears in chapter 1 and again, word for word, in chapter 3; the repeat is deliberate and works as a pin. The fixed sequence (field, principle, run, what I refused, court, where the field leads, limit, in your hands) is visible by chapter 3; **The court** and **Where the field leads** are where a decision-maker's attention dips.

**The author at his best.**
- "The name is still under the change. It certifies less." (chapter 2)
- "Judging authors does not keep pace with an afternoon. Judging changes can." (chapter 2)
- "Nobody had to read a diff to see that sixty-one per cent is not a penalty cap a bank may charge." (chapter 1)

**Least like him.** The scene-form paragraph inside **The slip** (chapter 1): a manual's voice, at the moment the story should hold its breath.

**What a reader who stops here takes away.** That the thing to govern is the change and not whoever wrote it; that a change can be declared, judged with a reason, recorded and taken back, and that it ran; and, honestly stated, that today the reach shown is a line, the origin is what the proposer declares, the reason lives only at the moment of judgement, and a text edit outside the gate is not caught by the library. A head of compliance leaves convinced of the direction and with three precise questions for Part II, which is the right place to leave him, provided the attribution and the "visible" items above are answered or pointed forward.
