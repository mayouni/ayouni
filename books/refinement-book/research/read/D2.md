# Reading D2: chapters 12, 13, the coda and the glossary

Group D2, 2026-10-15. Read as the author, after skimming `00-start-here.md` and `01-the-letter.md`; the four pages read whole, in order, then again with the brief's questions. Nothing was run. Two cross-references were checked against the Softanza book's pages (chapter 29, appendix D). Both hold.

## 12-what-the-auditor-saw.md

1. **What I remember.** Eighteen months on, the auditor asks who approved the change, what it affected and how the letters matched. The library answers the first from a saved `.zgov` file: three decisions, each with its actor, its authority at the time and its risk. It answers the second in part. For the third it points at a rule in the code. The proof is the round trip, save then load back. The limit is that the file keeps neither the timeline, nor the rule, nor the refusal, nor an approver distinct from the actor.

2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Under "The visit": "In the Teaching Edition of this book, Amina typed for a few seconds". The Teaching Edition is never introduced in 00 or 01. It first appears in chapter 6 with no gloss, and it is not in the glossary. By chapter 12 a reader may follow it, but a reader who opens Part IV directly will not. Its role here (the earlier edition drew the screen, and this one runs the file) deserves one clause, ideally in 00.
   - Under "Outside the story": "It uses the name Softibank for that bank too, and says the name is fictional." I had to read this paragraph twice. Chapter 1 says Softibank is invented. Here the same name is a pseudonym for a real bank. The paragraph then says the two histories are separate. All three statements are true, but together they read as a knot. One sentence would untie it: "the same name, given there to a real bank, here to an invented one."

3. **Where I doubted.**
   - Under "Outside the story": "The evidence of its use at a bank is still to come." The Softanza book's chapter 29 tells a product delivered and installed at a bank under this regulator. Put that way, the sentence sells the work short. The more exact sentence is the stage and the direction: the library's assessment runs at a bank today, and the refinement discipline at a bank is the next step. This is item 1 for the author.
   - Under "What the record answers, and what it does not": the three answers say "not" or "in part" seven times in four paragraphs. Each "not" is true and is said again in "The limit". The section reads as a ledger of absences rather than a record that answers the hardest part of the question, *who acted, under what authority, at the time*, and answers it from a file nobody wrote by hand. I would keep the facts and lead each answer with what it does.
   - "The report filed three weeks later was clean. That is fiction, and I mark it as such." The whole story is already declared fiction in chapter 1, so this second marking makes the reader wonder what else is not. It is good discipline, but it could be one clause shorter.

4. **What moved me.** "the file records what was true, not what is" (The court). That is the auditor's guarantee in nine words. I was also moved by "A record a person writes afterwards is missing the week it is needed." The section "The field" is short and does its job. I skipped nothing.

5. **The code.** The first block is announced well ("made on a governed code, and at the end the governance is saved"). The chain `SetRiskFor(...).SetAllowRefine(...).SetAuthorityLevel(...)` links plain calls rather than Q forms. If the house rule CODESTYLE-02 from the Softanza book binds this book too, these lines need a check, since the block already sits in a scene and separate lines would do. The second block's outputs (`3`, `compliance-officer`, `autonomous`, `3`, `2`) are exactly what the paragraph after it reads. The exercise `? read(cFile)` depends on `cFile` from the earlier block, so a reader running it alone gets nothing. It should say "after the first block".

6. **Errors of fact or form.**
   - "Chapter 7 said the timeline is not saved": consistent with chapter 1's "absent".
   - The September-to-March arithmetic ("the September before last", eighteen months) holds.
   - None sure beyond the above.

7. **The door.** The decision-maker. The two lines worth the walk are the principle ("written by the act of changing… outlives the program") and the court's last sentence. Yes, worth it.

8. **Verdict.** Ready after the items above. The "still to come" sentence and the Softibank double-naming need the author's word.

## 13-the-environment-specified.md

1. **What I remember.** Refine is the product on the foundation. It is specified in 113 documents, drawn in a prototype, and started as a command whose three verbs run, with six more that answer they are queued. The point is that the discipline lives in the library, so the gate stays the library's. The proof is the command, run on 15 October 2026, and one block of the library's verdicts. The limit is that Refine does not yet run as a product.

2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - Under "What runs today": "The command is its own ledger, and it reads the same one this paragraph does." I read it three times. I take it to mean the command reports from the same plan the paragraph was written from. As written, it is a riddle.
   - Under "What is specified": "(its section 5)". This comes after a sentence whose subject is "the specification". Which specification: the graph model's? A reader cannot tell, and cannot look it up (see 3).
   - Under "What runs today": "Refine's own `validate` is specified as a pipeline of four stages, without the library's governance and trust checks". This is dropped in without a word on why, or whether the product will gain them. Chapter 5 tells the history of four stages becoming five, and a clause pointing there ("the design chapter 5 tells, before the code grew to five") would turn a doubt into a direction.

3. **Where I doubted.**
   - Under "In your hands": "The specification's sections 5 and 6 frame an answer… Write yours before you read theirs." The limit names "a public repository" as absent. The reader cannot read theirs. Either say where the specification can be read, or turn the sentence into "the specification frames an answer; the library does not yet have one". This is a sure error of form.
   - Under "The principle": "If Refine did not exist, every block in this book would run as it did." It is true, and it is the right principle. Next to "The field leads Refine everywhere a product is used today", though, the chapter risks reading as an apology for its own subject. The author's rule is stage and direction. The section "What is specified" is the place to say plainly what Refine adds that no tool in the field has: one machinery, one gate as the one writer, across three worlds. It says so only once, in "Where the field leads".
   - Under "The limit": "The platform's site says the same in one word: the implementation's stage is 'specification'." It is accurate, but closing the chapter's limit on the word "specification" leaves the weakest note last. The built command and the 113 documents are the stronger last word.

4. **What moved me.** "A screen can draw these answers well. It does not need to make them." That sentence makes the whole principle concrete. The paragraph on twelve primitives "applied transactionally: all commit, or none" is the most precise thing in the chapter. "What is shown" bored me slightly: the prototype is described, but nothing in it is shown, not even one figure named.

5. **The code.** The one block follows its sentence and prints what chapter 1 already showed, the `0.61` refusal and the `0.16` admission. It is a deliberate echo and works as a "verdict card". The exercise fence `haro exercise` holds only comments in a notation that is not Haro (`refinement.set_value  penalty_cap  0.18 -> 0.16`). A reader who runs it gets nothing. Either tag it as a plain sketch or say "write it, do not run it".

6. **Errors of fact or form.**
   - "the equality of chapter 11, two points that must move together" is right: chapter 11 runs the equality as the rule the gate cannot pass one point at a time.
   - The stage words do not match the glossary. The front matter puts Refine's command under **built**, while the glossary defines built as "the code exists, a guard exists and a block ran". No guard covers the command, and no block in the book runs it ("this book did not run them", of its tests). Either the glossary's definition or this chapter's use needs a word.

7. **The door.** The decision-maker. The principle line ("never becomes the subject of the gate") is worth the walk. The "What runs today" paragraph is the second line, and it is held back by the ledger riddle.

8. **Verdict.** Ready after the items above. The unreadable specification in the exercise and the riddle sentence are the blockers. The tone of the limit needs the author's eye.

## 14-coda-the-next-letter.md

1. **What I remember.** A new letter in June. Amina writes the proposal, schedules the review, and puts the letter in the drawer. The letters have stopped being emergencies. The discipline is not a story: every refusal came from a library you can run. The book ends on the back-cover sentence.

2. **Where I lost the thread.** "a refinement of the rule, under compliance authority." In this book a refinement moves a point, and a rule is a derivation across points. "Refinement of the rule" blurs the two terms the book took twelve chapters to separate. Chapter 11 does quote the Teaching Edition saying the deepest change "was to the rule", so the phrase may be deliberate. It would read cleaner as "a refinement of the ratio, under compliance authority".

3. **Where I doubted.** "what each change reached". The coda's own front matter marks that cascade as designed. Inside the fiction it is fair, and the front matter says so. I note it only because the coda's last paragraph claims "every limit is said".

4. **What moved me.** "It is not that the letters stopped mattering. It is that they stopped being emergencies." It is the best two-sentence verdict in my group. The echo "On a Tuesday in June" against chapter 1's "a Tuesday in September" lands without being pointed at. Nothing bored me. At 229 words, the coda is the right length.

5. **The code.** None, rightly.

6. **Errors of fact or form.** None sure. The front matter has no `part:`, which is consistent with 00 and 15.

7. **The door.** The educator: the sentence on letters and emergencies is the one to give a class. Worth it.

8. **Verdict.** Ready. "Refinement of the rule" is the author's call.

## 15-glossary.md

1. **What I remember.** Forty-odd words, each tied to the chapter that runs it. Each one agrees with the Softanza book's appendix D or says where it parts from it (checkpoint, gate, twin). The stage words are defined. The gate's two orders (the code's and the message's) are said once.

2. **Where I lost the thread.**
   - "A word names a state of a piece of work or a role held in a scope, never a person." The entries include a bank (Softibank), a language (Haro), a product (Refine) and "actor… a person, a program or an AI agent". The rule, as written, is contradicted by the page it heads. It probably means that no entry names a real person. Say that.
   - **gate.** "five checks, in the order the code runs them, structural, constraint, governance, trust and derivation; the admitted message lists them in another order." This is clear. But no entry says governance is the third check, while constraint, trust and derivation each give their place. For symmetry, the entry **governance** should add "the third check of the gate".

3. **Where I doubted.** **built.** "the code exists, a guard exists and a block ran." This is stricter than 00's own definition ("the code exists and a block ran"). Chapter 13 marks Refine's command as built with no guard and no block (see 13, item 6). One of the two needs to give way.

4. **What moved me.** **origin, or posture.** "Declared, not proven." Three words that carry the honesty of chapter 8. Nothing bored me, since a glossary is read by entry.

5. **The code.** None.

6. **Errors of fact or form.**
   - Alphabetical order is broken: **refinement** comes before **Refine**. Sorted case-blind, the order is refinable code, Refine, refinement, refinement point.
   - Missing entries for words every chapter uses or my group leans on:
     - **court**, a heading in every chapter;
     - **Teaching Edition**;
     - the three forms **classic / chain / scene**, from 00;
     - **transformation** and **primitive**, from chapter 13;
     - **governed code**, from chapter 12.
   - Cross-references to Softanza's appendix D for checkpoint, gate, refinement and twin are checked and correct.

7. **The door.** The AI agent: a glossary that pins each word to a chapter is exactly what an AI agent reading the book needs. Worth it.

8. **Verdict.** Ready after the items above. The opening rule and the definition of built are the two that matter.

## The group

**The thread.** Chapter 12 closes Part IV on its bridge: "Part IV ends with the auditor gone and the report filed." Chapter 13 opens on exactly that turn. Its last line, "A coda returns to Softibank, for the next letter", lands on the coda's first sentence, and the coda's last sentence returns to chapter 1's principle. The book closes in a circle, and well. Part V is one chapter, so it reads less as a part than as an epilogue to the library. Its title, "specified", says so honestly.

**The author at his best.**
- "the file records what was true, not what is" (12, The court)
- "A screen can draw these answers well. It does not need to make them." (13, What runs today)
- "It is not that the letters stopped mattering. It is that they stopped being emergencies." (14)

**Least like him.** Chapter 13, "The command is its own ledger, and it reads the same one this paragraph does." It is clever where the rest of the voice is plain. Close behind is the run of "not" and "in part" in chapter 12's "What the record answers, and what it does not", which reads as a hedge rather than as stage and direction.

**What a reader who stops here takes away.** Every change to the cap was judged before it landed, and the decision outlived the program that made it. Refine is the product being built on top, honestly dated. Govern the change, not the intelligence. A reader also takes away a slight sense that the book's last two chapters say more about what is missing than about what was achieved. That is the one note the author should rebalance before he puts his name on it.
