## 1. Shape (C9)

Opening: first person, three sentences, says what the chapter does and where it stops: "I close on what that one run does not yet do." Good. The second paragraph (the fiction declared) sits between the roadmap and the scene; it is required (ROP-05) and short.

Section closes: **The letter** closes on a question beat ("When a change is written, what else moves?"), a hand-over. **The field** closes on "the letter has a date on it", a hand-over. **The cap, declared** closes on a defensive aside ("and I print the run"), a dead end. **The court** closes on "The chapters of Part II run what they prove", a hand-over.

Chapter close: a bridge ("The next chapter asks why one change became hard"). Not a summary.

## 2. Run, then read (C1)

The run order is broken once, in the place that matters most. Under **The slip**, the block's exact answer is followed not by its reading but by a paragraph on code forms: "The block is written as a scene: the object is named once, `o { ... }`". The reading ("Refused! The refusal names its check first") comes one paragraph later, and the exclamation loses its run. The forms note belongs before the block or in a short aside after the reading.

Worst abstract paragraph: under **The field**, "A diff tells you which lines changed. It does not tell you that the line was meant to change..." holds, because the letter's scene is within reach. Under **The cap, declared**, the paragraph after the second block explains three undeclared checks before any of them is shown: "no governance, floor of trust or rule across points was declared yet" (dense, five clauses, no figure).
