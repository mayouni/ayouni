# Close reading — chapters 2 and 3, "Data types in Ring" and "Ins of Ring"

Read 2026-10-11 by a fresh reader (an agent given the extracted text and `STYLE-BRIEF.md`, nothing else). Both extracts are drafts with dropped words (ch03 much more), so some evidence is partial.

**1. Shape of a chapter**

Opening, ch02: (a) personal career observation turned into a thesis — "I made an observation of a general symptom of low quality code: poor mastery of data type systems"; (b) a complaint about other books and a promise — "Decidedly, it won't be the case of this Ring book"; (c) a full roadmap paragraph in "I" ("In this chapter, I take you in a discovery…", "In the second section, I expose…"). Opening, ch03: (a) an aphorism — "A programming language is a factory for transforming inputs to outputs. Ring isn't an exception."; (b) the roadmap, this time for three chapters; (c) a rhetorical question answered in two words — "Without data, what a computer program can do? Nothing really."

Build: ch02 is a survey — `#` sections per theme, `##` per type, each a 1–3 paragraph lead, a short listing (4–15 lines), then "Run it"/"Test it". ch03 is one continuous worked project (northafricaApp) announced as "this 7-steps script" and carried across all sections; listings there are long (datagraph.ring ≈ 100 lines, most of it `#` comments that narrate: "# Let's start with an empty matrix"). Code almost always comes after a one-line colon lead ("like this:", "Not difficult:") and explanation lives in trailing comments ("// Gives 20"). ch02 repeats a "complete listing" after each incremental build (§ User-defined data types, twice). Ratio: ch02 roughly 55/45 prose/code; ch03 nearer 40/60.

Fixed parts: none formal (no summary, no numbered exercises). Instead embedded challenges: "Challenge: play with this code to force Ring raise the following error" (§ More on Numbers), "If you prefer close the book and take it as an exercise…" (§ User-defined data types), "If so, this is your penalty:" (§ Final refinement). Figures are frequent and captioned with jokes ("Figure 2.– Ring is a citizen of the world!"). Close: ch02 ends in a theory section with a Persian proverb mapped onto the book's chapters ("the current one as the second step with the good word"); ch03 ends on a challenge and a trailing fragment — no closure.

**2. Teaching moves**

Example before name, consistently: values n1–n5 are shown, then "are all of type number" (§ Meet the Gang of Four); the matrix is built in Excel terms and only then named — "the matrix is the datagraph" (§ Ins from text files); the `[]` need is established, then "bienvenue to operator overloading!".
Error first, then diagnosis: "You run the code and you get an angry error message" (§ Converting between data types); the natural-language program is run broken first ("the following 4 error messages are displayed", § A little bit of magic).
Deliberate bug left for the reader: "did you run the code and discovered this error message? … Correct it please." (§ Converting).
Question-first when a new term arrives: "Wndering what are these 'datagraphs' thing? … All are good questions." (§ Ins from text files).
Limits stated plainly: "Ring strings are coded in 8 bits… not suitable to manage Unicode text like Arabic"; "exceeds the scope of this book"; "list2code()… doesn't help either"; "A feature I didn't covered here"; "performance, which I agree on" (§ Capturing the potential). Confusions pre-empted by negative definition: "Implicit conversion shouldn't be confused with implicit declaration… Nor… with explicit conversion" (§ Implicit conversion).
ch03 adds a design-reasoning move absent from ch02: a working system is declared insufficient and refactored ("Seems we achieved our goal, but we didn't", § Ins from database), ending in connector/transformer/datagraph/renderer.

**3. Voice and address**

"I" = experience and opinion: "In the course of my career", "My personal opinion is", "For me the result is awesome", "Personally, I would go a little further" (ch03 ×2). "We" = doing it together: "we craft the following lines", "we can congratulate ourselves". "You" = instructions and checks: "Save it as ucdstring.ring." The programmer is "she/her" throughout: "weave any useful carpet with her program code", "by the programmer herself". Ring is "she": "She is not against freedom" (§ A little bit of magic).
Direct questions as beats: "Happy? And so am I.", "Agreed?", "Right?", "Still wandering?", "Tired? I understand.", "Awesome bar chart, isn't it?", "clean is clean, isn't it?". Exclamations frequent: "Lists ô lists!", "I told you it was a lot of work!", "And oplà!", "Ring gets an immediate headache and gets a stack overflow!". Imperatives short and stacked: "Execute, play and play again.", "Run it.", "Execute. It's functional."
Humour: mock-grave fables ("Maiga should be prepared to a supreme punishment"), self-mockery ("Its ok if you feel I'm emotional"), threats in jest ("Tell me it wasn't that beautiful, and I'll feel disappointed…"). Grave only in the Reminder note ("your unbeatable arm for making efficient and bug-free programs") and the freedom line.

**5. Sentence and paragraph craft**

Long setup then short punch: "…One needs to make this in object-oriented style? A lot." One-sentence/one-word paragraphs: "Ring is written in ANSI C.", "Excellent.", "So natural.", "Now.". Colon before every listing; parentheses for asides "(the value of nothing!)", "(normal! You won't rely on the sky…)"; closing ellipses "…" on section ends (×5). Characteristic words (both chapters): *Let's* (~12), *witch* for which (~11), *just* (~11), *simply* (~8), *craft* (6), *world* (program/screen world, 7), *Visually speaking* (4), *In fact* (4), *hole* for whole (4), *awesome* (3), *without a hassle* (2), *Personally* (2). Code names in prose are bare: "add a load "connector.ring" line", "aResult[], the list returned". Editor cuts: the opener's "by the force of circumstances, language of the language defining the nomenclature"; repeated full listings; hedges "number() is maybe what we need"; fact slips — "Stack overflow error" for a *Numeric* Overflow, "the float type of C" after "Ring always use Double", Laurence Tratt called "she" and placed at Bournemouth (verify).

**6. Structure of explanation**

Concrete → general: four listings, then "Therefore, types are what conveys meaning". Principle usually extracted after, except the Reminder/Note boxes, which state it flatly. Tables for reference (operators, is* functions, typed-language quadrant); bullet lists for steps and skills ("These ones are the strict minimum:"). Facts sourced and dated: "Data are taken from Wolfram|Alfa witch compiled them from The World Factook", "DATA OF 2015-2017", Wikipedia quoted twice, Harper and Tratt cited, "BigNumber library was developed by Bert Mariani and Zsolt", counts "22 mathematic functions", "16 standard functions". Honesty markers: "I'm invited to leave you alone in this complementary exercise", "based on my practical programming experience".

**8. What an AI would get wrong**

Easy to fake badly: (a) the exclamations and "isn't it?" tags — sprinkled, they become cute; his are always placed after a run or a reveal; (b) the proverbs and foreign words — an AI would pick generic ones; his are specific, personal (own wife, daughters' ages, Timbuktu, Niger, Mali francs) and sometimes slightly off, which is the authenticity; (c) tidy prose — the voice depends on French-shaped English ("in plus of", "I'm invited to leave you alone"), which polishing erases.
Hard-to-fake core: he puts real stakes in the reader's hands — leaves a bug unfixed, assigns a "penalty", refuses to finish the second chart ("It depends on the strategy you adopt"); and he smuggles architecture (separation of concerns, connector/renderer refactoring, a typed-language quadrant with Harper) into a beginner chapter, treating the beginner as a future professional.
