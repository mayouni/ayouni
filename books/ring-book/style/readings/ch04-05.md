**1. Shape of a chapter**

Openings differ. Ch04 opens with a three-part bodily analogy ("When you speak… When you move… When you code, you are exposing the data"), then a one-sentence "In this chapter, I show you how…" plan, then a continuity link ("we build on [what] we achieved in the previous chapter"), then a cast: "Sam and Dan are two programmers of the northafricaApp team", with scripted dialogue ("Sam: Exactly! Ch05 opens with a definition ("designing an algorithm… turns back to transforming inputs to outputs"), then the plan, then a self-rating ("this chapter forms the heart of the book"), then a numbered roadmap by section ("In sections 4 and 5, we go deeper…").

Sections: ch04 = one project (northafricaApp) advanced one output file at a time (out2screen/out2file/out2db/out2web/out2pdf), 4 headings; ch05 = 25 headings, concept per section, each with a daily-life narrative → Drakon diagram → Ring code → comparison. Fixed parts: ch05 has a declared closing homework ("§ Before you leave: a homework!") and named rules ("my three commandments"); ch04 has no summary heading but a summary paragraph ("To summarize it, three options of rendering the chart are now implemented"), a teaser ("A very exciting chapter is waiting for you!") and a personal coda (see §7). **2. Teaching moves**

Example first, then a question, then the rule (ch04 § opening: `? "Apress"` / `SEE "Editions"` → "But what if you inverse the order…? Why?" → "Because, while the two keywords are equivalent…"). Wrong version first, staged: ch04 § Out to a web page runs three failing attempts ("works but is visually unsatisfying", "You see? The new line is completely ignored", "Same result.") before "Finally, it's resolved". Ch05 § Selection shows the right code then two *working* versions he rejects: "I would reject an equivalent version… just because the golden happy path rule is broken" — he teaches taste, not syntax. Ch05 names before showing when it is his own idea ("I define the problem of learnability as the inability of a programmer to distill the logical flow"), after showing when it is the language's (for, while, do again). Reader's mistake anticipated: "You may think now that we are going to use the [Load] command… but we don't" (ch04); the infinite while: "program run continually until computer is completely out of memory!" (ch05 § while). Incremental method made explicit: "securing a small logical achievement in every incremental step" — happy path first, ELSE blocks added one level at a time, reader runs the file each time. Limits/ugliness: "a dangerous feature a programmer should use with a sense of responsibility" (ch04 ChangeRingKeyword); "Intellectually, I may be satisfied, but artistically nope" (ch04 web); exit/loop: "many voices in the programming would go against this practice… Which is wise, but Ring is a pragmatic, realistic and free language" (ch05 § Exiting).

**8. What an AI would get wrong**

Easy to fake badly: the cheer ("So nice! Let's go on.", "yalla!") sprinkled as seasoning; Tunisian/Arabic references dropped in as colour; Sam/Dan dialogue as decoration rather than as the carrier of design decisions. Hard-to-fake core: (a) rejecting *correct* code on visual/moral grounds and saying "I would reject… just because"; (b) the slow experimental staging where the reader runs a failing version two or three times before the fix, and builds the happy path before any ELSE; (c) personal time stamped into the book (1989 MSX, a cousin's medal "today") unconnected to the topic; (d) the non-native texture (calques, "nope", "dear friend", comic hedge-stacks) — an AI will over-correct into fluent English and lose it; the author must decide which of these are him and which are errors.
