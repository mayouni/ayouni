**HOUSE-11 · the argument, as the author agreed with it, kept as the reason.** "Pay when you find it useful" is a promise of pay *after* use; a PDF behind payment is pay *before* use and reintroduces the thing the model removes. The PDF readers (offline, annotating, printing a chapter for a colleague, bringing it into a meeting) are the ones in whom "I found it useful" happens; gating the PDF gates that moment. The gate is not a lock anyway: the reader is one self-contained HTML file, anyone can save or print it; a wall anyone can walk around protects nothing and signals distrust. The house is open everywhere else (readers for people, data for AI agents, public source, every block runnable); a closed PDF would be the one closed door, and the one a decision-maker notices. Pay-what-you-want yields little money by itself; its currency is reach and trust, which for books addressed to professional decision-makers is the right currency: the book sells the platform and the practice, the paper and the author's time are the products. The free-online, buy-the-paper pattern has a long record (Eloquent JavaScript, Pro Git, SICP): the paper copy is bought as an object by people who already read the free one.
